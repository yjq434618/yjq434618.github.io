Hall 1

The Dawn of Computing

Long before electricity, humans were already thinking about "how to calculate." Click on the years to expand details.

c. 2400 BCThe Abacus▶

One of humanity's earliest calculating tools. By representing values through the position of beads, it is still used in some commercial settings today and is hailed as "the world's oldest computer."

1642Pascal's Calculator▶

Built by 19-year-old Blaise Pascal to help his father calculate taxes, it was the world's first mechanical adding machine, utilizing a gear-driven carry mechanism.

1837Babbage's Analytical Engine▶

A general-purpose mechanical computer designed by Charles Babbage, featuring input, storage, processing, and output structures. Ada Lovelace wrote programs for it and is recognized as the world's first programmer.

1936The Turing Machine▶

An abstract computational model proposed by Alan Turing that defined the boundaries of "computability." It is the theoretical foundation of modern computer science, and the Turing Award is named after him.

Hall 2

The Electronic Computer Era

Vacuum tubes, transistors, and integrated circuits—three leaps that made machines smaller and faster.

⚡

ENIAC (1946)

The first general-purpose electronic digital computer. Weighing 30 tons and covering 167 square meters, it used 18,000 vacuum tubes and could perform 5,000 additions per second.

◧

The Transistor (1947)

Invented at Bell Labs, it replaced bulky and fragile vacuum tubes, drastically reducing computer size and greatly improving reliability, ushering in the second generation of computers.

▦

Integrated Circuit (1958)

Jack Kilby and Robert Noyce independently implemented the integration of multiple components onto a single semiconductor chip. Moore's Law took off, and computing power began to grow exponentially.

▤

IBM System/360 (1964)

The first computer family to achieve software compatibility. It established the architectural paradigm of modern computers, an influence that persists today.

Hall 3

Personal Computers & The Internet

Computers moved from labs to desks, and from standalone machines to global connectivity.

🖥

Apple II (1977)

The first mass-market successful personal computer, allowing ordinary people to own a computer for the first time.

⌨

IBM PC (1981)

Its open architecture spawned the entire PC-compatible industry, establishing the dominance of the x86 ecosystem.

🪟

Graphical User Interface

Originating at Xerox PARC and popularized by Apple and Microsoft, the mouse and windows completely transformed human-computer interaction.

🌐

World Wide Web (1989)

Tim Berners-Lee proposed the WWW at CERN and made the standards freely available to all humanity.
